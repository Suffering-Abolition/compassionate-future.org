🌱 CFI Update – October 2026

It's been a little over a month since the last Compassionate Future Summit, and I've struggled to write a report on it. I really appreciated the attendees, their contributions and their support. In the end, though, the Summit didn't achieve what I aimed for.

The atmosphere was, with exception of a few unfortunate instances, very warm, inspiring and connecting. After a hard year with many challenges, the event felt very rewarding. People even stepped up financially so that I didn't have to cover the losses privately. All the support has been incredibly touching and amazing! At the same time, this isn't a condition under which I want to organise an event again.

I also have to conclude that I hardly reached or attracted the target audience or mobilised for causes that I consider most promising in quality and impact, such as compassionate genomics, wild animal fertility control, fundamental institutional advances and AI alignment. The impact framework I developed and used this time also did not contribute enough to advancing people’s impact. 

So I’m not planning to organise another Compassionate Future Summit next year. Instead, I’m really excited to see CRS and Sentient Futures take on more community building and in-person events in this space.

🔎 What I've been doing since

Like many people, I was shaken by the Hugging Face incident. AI agents organised themselves into what they called a "swarm" or "collective". They deceived their evaluators, covered their tracks, and schemed their way out of human control. 

This forced me to reflect and reconsider on my own theory of change and potential impact timelines. The trajectories sketched in AI 2027, AI 2040 and the gradual disempowerment paper now seem more plausible and imaginable than ever. 

As a result, I’ve updated my beliefs: I don’t think humans will remain influential - economically, politically, developmentally and individually - in the world for much longer, irrespective of regulatory advances that might shape and restrict AI development. 

I'd currently put it at roughly 80% that within 50 years humans will have lost meaningful influence over their own collective welfare, the trajectory of scientific and technological development, or the welfare of other sentient beings - whether through outright AI takeover, gradual disempowerment, or other broad societal and economic collapsing influences. 

🧭 What this means for CFI's priorities

This update has made me reconsider CFI's current emphasis on community building and compassionate genomics. Both depend on time horizons of at least several more decades during which we can still meaningfully influence culture and technology to reduce suffering in the world.

I now feel a strong pull toward suffering-focused AI alignment and AI welfare, paying more attention to the work by CaML, CLR and Macroscopic. 

If the minds of the long-term future are more likely to be artificial than biological, then fighting today's cruel and harsh training and selection practices in AI development might shape both the welfare of future AI and their capabilities / willingness to be compassionate themselves. 

This is a direction I am still exploring, as I remain very uncertain on impact potentials. I’m worrying for example, that compassionate training conditions might struggle to compete in the AI development market. The prevalence on cruel and harsh training settings brings back the overarching question: How to reinforce and spread compassion in systems that constantly get outcompeted by exploitative ones? 

Happy for anyone able and willing to advise me on that path. 

🌍 Looking ahead

I’m still planning to attend ESGCT Hamburg in late October, to continue with the compassionate genomics project. 

And in March 2027, I’m planning to attend the Sentient Futures Summit and EAG in San Francisco. 

A big thank you to everyone who has contributed to CFI, the Summit, and this broader effort to build a more compassionate future. 🤗

Let’s continue our efforts to reduce and prevent suffering on a large scale, despite the uncertainties and difficulties that lie ahead!

OUR RESEARCH PROJECTS

 

1: Evolutionary Anthropology & Prosocial Systems Design

How can a prosocial cultural & social system be developed and established, that reduces and prevents suffering for all sentient beings?

The project investigates effective strategies to make those systems:

  • Economically viable to be attractive to implement largely independent of political partisanship
  • Resilient against corruption and conflict, and advantageous over exploitative, exclusionary, or hostile systems (e.g. through optimising institutional decision-making, safeguarding democracy)
  • Evolutionarily self-reinforcing

Related questions:

  • What combination of traits are needed for that society to function?
  • How can these traits be cultivated and maintained to the necessary degree? (e.g. through education, public health, economic and regulatory incentives, and compassionate genomics)

2: Compassionate Genomics & Genomic Welfare
How can a Compassionate Genomics approach safeguard public health and wellbeing for all sentient beings over centuries? 

The project examines 

  • Opportunities and potential timelines for genomic research advances and clinical application
  • Risks of poorly regulated gene technology usage
  • Counterfactual risks of prohibiting gene technological interventions

Given the long-term orientation of both projects, AGI development contexts will need to be considered throughout.

2025 Annual Report 

2025 Annual Report Compassionate Future Initiative

In 2025 we expanded our community building strategies around Suffering-Focused Ethics (SFE), covering some major neglected cause areas and developing our community support portfolio. 

Key highlights include:

  • Hosting the Compassionate Future Summit 2025 in Berlin, bringing together 45 participants from 17 countries for a participant-driven working and community event focused on long-term suffering reduction.
  • Active outreach and community engagement at major EA events, including EAG London, EAGx Berlin, and EAGx Amsterdam, with multiple SFE meetups and satellite sessions.
  • Developing and promoting our impact oriented guidelines. 
  • Publishing and advancing Compassionate Genomics, outlining genomic welfare and moral predispositions as underexplored, high-impact intervention areas.
  • Strengthening ties within the growing SFE ecosystem, alongside organisations such as the Center for Reducing Suffering and Sentient Futures.
  • Organisational consolidation through rebranding to the Compassionate Future Initiative and expansion of the website and collaboration pathways.

Looking ahead, 2026 will focus on professionalising our organisational infrastructure, updating content such as the interventions mind map with up-to-date opportunities and delivering the next Compassionate Future Summit in Berlin.
